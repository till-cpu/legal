Spearfish

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 October 2026

Spearfish is a spearfishing app: it records your dive spots, your catches and your dives, and shows you sea conditions. This policy explains what it stores, where it goes, and how to get rid of it.

It is written to be read. If something here is unclear, ask — the address is at the bottom.

The short version

  • Spearfish has no analytics, no advertising and no trackers. Nothing is collected to profile you or sold to anyone.
  • Your spots are private by default and are never shown to other users unless you deliberately make one public or share it.
  • Your data lives in a Supabase database hosted in Seoul, South Korea.
  • You can delete your account and everything in it from inside the app, at any time, without asking anyone.

Who is responsible for your data

Spearfish is made by Till Louesse, an individual developer based in the United Arab Emirates. He decides what Spearfish does with your data and is responsible for it — in the language of data protection law, the controller. The address to write to is at the bottom of this page.

What Spearfish stores

Your account — email address, a username, and a password. The password is hashed by our authentication provider and is never visible to us. If you sign in with Google instead, we receive your email address and name from Google; we never see your Google password.

Your profile — optional. A display name, a photo, your region, diving certifications, and Instagram or YouTube handles if you choose to add them.

Your spots — a name, coordinates, depth, bottom type and your notes. Spots are private by default. A spot becomes visible to others only if you switch it to public.

Your catches — species, weight, length, depth, date, your notes, and a photo if you add one.

Your dives — start and end time, the return time you set, the buddy you selected, the gear you chose, and — only if you turn on track recording — a position roughly once a minute while the app is open.

If a dive runs overdue — when you have not ended or extended a dive 15 minutes after the return time you set, the buddy you chose for that dive is sent an alert with your name, how late you are, and your last recorded position if you were recording your track. When you then end or extend the dive, they are told you have checked in. Nobody else is told, and nothing is sent for a dive where you chose no buddy — you get a reminder at your return time, and that is all.

Your messages and buddies — buddy requests, groups you belong to, and the messages you send. Messages are stored so they can be delivered and re-read.

Your devices — if you allow notifications, a push token for each phone you sign in on. It is an address for delivering alerts, such as a buddy’s dive running overdue, and is used for nothing else. It is removed when you sign out on that phone, and a phone that has not opened Spearfish for 60 days is forgotten.

On your phone — so the app works where there is no signal, it keeps a copy of your spots, catches, buddies and gear on your phone, and holds anything you save at sea — a spot, a catch and its photo, a dive and its track — until it can be sent. That copy is removed when you delete your account.

Reports and blocks — if you report something, the report records who sent it, whose content it is, the reason, and a copy of the reported message or entry, so it can still be reviewed if the original is deleted. Only the developer sees reports, and the person reported is never told who reported them. If you block someone, that is stored too, and only you can see it.

Location

Spearfish asks for location for four things:

  • Sea conditions — to show swell, wind and tide where you are. Your coordinates are rounded to about 1.1 km before being sent to the forecast provider, so the request never carries your precise position.
  • Showing you on the chart — your position centres the map and is drawn on it, on your phone. It is not stored or sent; the map providers listed below only see which area of the map is on your screen.
  • Recording a dive track — only when you switch it on for a dive.
  • Putting a catch on the buddies map — only if you tick that box for a specific catch. Coordinates are rounded to about 1.1 km on your device before they are stored, so the exact spot never leaves your phone.

Location is collected only while the app is open. Spearfish does not track you in the background.

Who else sees your data

Other Spearfish users see only what you deliberately share:

  • catches you submit to the leaderboard (your name, the species and the weight)
  • catches you tick onto the buddies map, with their photo (coarse location only, to accepted buddies only)
  • spots you switch to public, or share with a specific buddy
  • messages you send, to their recipients
  • if a dive runs overdue, the buddy you chose for it: your name, how late you are, and your last recorded position
  • your profile — display name, photo, region and level are visible to other signed-in users

Photos

Catch photos are private. Only you can open them — plus your accepted buddies, for a catch you choose to show on the buddies map, and only while it stays there. The app fetches each photo with your own sign-in, so a copied link opens nothing on its own.

Profile pictures are different. Other users see yours, and it is stored at a public link: anyone who has that direct link can open the image, even without a Spearfish account. The link is long and not guessable, and the app never publishes it outside the app.

Why Spearfish is allowed to use it

  • To give you the app you signed up for — your account, spots, catches, dives, messages, and the buddy alerts you set up. Without this data there is no logbook to keep.
  • Because you said yes — location, camera, photos and notifications are each asked for by your phone, and you can take any of them back in its settings. The same goes for everything you choose to share: a public spot, a leaderboard entry, a catch on the buddies map.
  • To keep the service safe — for example, looking into a report that someone is misusing it.

Where it is stored

On Supabase’s servers in Seoul, South Korea. The European Commission and the United Kingdom both recognise South Korea as protecting personal data to their own standard, so moving your data there needs no further safeguard under their law.

How long it is kept

Your data is kept until you delete it. Deleting your account removes everything immediately and permanently. A report is kept for as long as it takes to deal with it.

Deleting your account

In the app: Me → Delete account. It asks twice, then removes your profile, spots, catches, photos, dives, tracks, gear, buddy connections, group memberships, messages, notification registrations, blocks and the reports you filed. This cannot be undone and we cannot recover it for you afterwards.

Children

Spearfish is not directed at children and is not intended for anyone under 16.

Your rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, export or erase your data, or to object to its processing. Most of this is available directly in the app; for anything else, write to the address below and we will respond.

If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom and think your data has been mishandled, you can complain to your country’s data protection authority. Writing to us first is usually the quicker way to get it put right.

Changes

If this policy changes materially, the app will say so before the change takes effect. The date at the top always reflects the current version.
